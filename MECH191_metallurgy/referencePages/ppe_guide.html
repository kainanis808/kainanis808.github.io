PPE Reference Guide

What to wear and why, from the general shop baseline through unit-specific requirements

Every unit in MECH191 puts students around a different hazard — hot metal, rotating spindles, corrosive etchants, arc flash, fumes. There's no single PPE loadout that covers all of it, and in a few places the wrong choice is actually more dangerous than none at all (gloves near a lathe chuck, for one). This page starts with what's required in the shop at all times, then breaks out what to add for each unit's specific activities.

General Shop PPE — Required at All Times

This is the floor, not the ceiling. Every activity below adds to this list; nothing on this page removes from it.

Eyes
  • ANSI Z87.1-rated safety glasses, worn at all times in the shop — even when only observing
Feet
  • Closed-toe, closed-heel shoes — no sandals, canvas shoes, or open-back footwear
Clothing
  • Long pants, no loose sleeves, drawstrings, or dangling jewelry
  • Long hair tied back and contained
General conduct
  • No headphones/earbuds that block shop announcements or machine alarms
  • Know the nearest eyewash station, safety shower, and fire extinguisher before starting work

Eye & Face Protection Tiers

Not all eye protection is interchangeable — the right tier depends on the hazard, not personal preference.

ProtectionStandard / RatingProtects AgainstRequired For
Safety glasses ANSI Z87.1 (basic impact) Flying particles, basic impact Baseline — worn everywhere in the shop
Safety glasses, Z87+ ANSI Z87+ (high impact) High-velocity impact (grinding, machining chips) Machining, grinding, abrasive cutting
Chemical splash goggles ANSI Z87.1 D3 splash rating Liquid splash — sealed frame, no gaps Mixing/using etchants, penetrant/developer chemicals, plating & pickling baths
Full face shield Worn over glasses/goggles, not alone Splash to the whole face, flying debris Kroll's reagent, molten metal pours, bench grinding
Welding helmet Auto-darkening or fixed shade — see shade chart below Arc flash, UV/IR radiation, spatter Any arc welding process — worn over Z87.1 safety glasses, not instead of them
UV-blocking eyewear UV-A absorbing lens UV-A exposure from the black light source Reading fluorescent liquid penetrant indications under UV-A

A face shield is always worn in addition to safety glasses or goggles, never as a replacement — it stops splash and debris from the front but leaves the sides of the eyes exposed on its own.

Hand Protection — Glove Selection

The right glove depends entirely on the hazard. Using the wrong type is the most common PPE mistake in a metals shop — and near rotating machinery, gloves themselves are the hazard.

Glove TypeUse ForDo Not Use For
Nitrile or neoprene, chemical-resistant Mixing/handling etchants, penetrant & developer chemicals, plating/pickling solutions Never latex for HF-containing etchants (Keller's, Kroll's) — latex does not reliably block HF
Heat-resistant (leather, Kevlar, or aluminized) Furnace/heat-treatment handling, casting pours, hot specimen handling Not chemical-resistant — don't use for etchant handling
Leather welding gauntlets Arc welding, oxyfuel cutting, grinding sparks Not rated for chemical splash
Cut-resistant Handling sheared/sharp sheet metal edges, freshly cut stock Not for chemical or high-heat contact
No gloves Operating any machine with an accessible rotating or moving part —
Never Wear Gloves Near Rotating Machinery
Lathes, drill presses, mills, sanders, jointers, and grinders can catch a glove and pull the hand in before there's time to react — documented incidents include partial finger and thumb amputations from exactly this. This is the one place in the shop where removing PPE is the correct safety action. Remove gloves before operating any machine with an accessible rotating or moving part, full stop.

PPE by Unit & Activity

Add these to the general shop baseline above for the specific activity underway.

Units 1–2 Hot Work & Heat Treatment

Furnace loading/unloading, quenching, temper-color work, pyrometer/heat-indicating-crayon use.

  • Heat-resistant gloves (leather or aluminized) rated for the furnace temperature in use
  • Face shield or safety glasses plus a heat-resistant apron for quenching, where splash/spatter is possible
  • No synthetic clothing (nylon, polyester) near furnaces or open flame — it can melt onto skin
  • Long sleeves, natural fiber (cotton) preferred, cuffs down and buttoned
Unit 2 Metallography Lab — Etchants

Mixing, swabbing, or immersion-etching any of the six lab etchants. Full etchant-by-etchant hazard detail is on the Etchant Safety Data Sheets page.

  • Nitrile or neoprene gloves — never latex — for all six etchants
  • Chemical splash goggles at minimum for all six etchants; Kroll's reagent specifically calls for a full face shield in addition to goggles
  • Mix and etch only in a fume hood — required for HF-containing reagents and for Nital's methanol vapor
  • Calcium gluconate gel staged at the etching station whenever Keller's or Kroll's reagent is in use
  • No open flame nearby — Nital (methanol) and Kalling's No. 2 (ethanol) are both flammable
Unit 3 Testing, Inspection & NDT

Tensile/hardness/impact testing, liquid penetrant and magnetic particle inspection.

  • Stay clear of pinch points and the load path on tensile, hardness, and impact (Charpy) testing machines — these store or release significant mechanical energy
  • Nitrile gloves for penetrant, developer, and remover chemicals
  • Chemical splash goggles when applying or removing penetrant/developer
  • UV-A blocking eyewear when reading fluorescent penetrant indications under black light — direct UV-A exposure to unprotected eyes is a real hazard, not just a discomfort
  • Adequate ventilation — many penetrant/remover formulations are solvent-based and flammable
Unit 4 Manufacturing Processes

Casting, machining, powder metallurgy, abrasive/grinding operations.

  • Casting: face shield over safety glasses, heat-resistant gloves, and a heat-resistant apron/gaiters for any molten-metal pour
  • Machining (lathes, mills, drill presses): Z87+ safety glasses, no gloves, no loose clothing or jewelry, hair contained — see the glove warning above
  • Grinding/abrasive finishing: face shield over Z87+ glasses, hearing protection, and a dust mask/respirator for fine particulate
  • Powder metallurgy: dust mask or respirator when handling loose metal powders — many are combustible in dust form
Unit 5 Welding, Corrosion & Modern Tech

Arc and oxyfuel welding, brazing/soldering, corrosion protection and coating work.

  • Welding helmet with the correct shade lens for the process and amperage in use (see chart below) — worn over Z87.1 safety glasses
  • Leather welding gauntlets and a flame-resistant jacket or apron; no synthetic clothing under welding leathers
  • Fume extraction or adequate ventilation — required whenever welding, and especially critical on galvanized/coated or stainless steel (zinc oxide fume, chromium/nickel fume)
  • Hearing protection where grinding or air-arc gouging accompanies the welding work
  • Chemical-resistant gloves and goggles for corrosion protection work involving plating, pickling, or acid-cleaning solutions

Know Before You Start: Emergency Equipment Locations

PPE reduces risk; it doesn't eliminate it. Locate these before starting any activity on this page, not after something goes wrong.

Confirm the location of: the nearest eyewash station and safety shower, the fire extinguisher rated for the material in use, the spill kit, and — for any session using Keller's or Kroll's reagent — the calcium gluconate gel.
