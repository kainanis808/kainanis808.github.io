ME580 Microscope & Etchant Reference

AmScope ME580T-PZ-2L — magnifications, field widths, and specimen etchants for the Metallography Lab modules

Every weekly module in the Semester Metallography Portfolio uses the same shared class microscope and a small set of etchants. This page is a single reference for both: what the ME580 can actually do, and what each lettered specimen (A–O) needs to be etched with before it goes on the stage.

The Microscope — AmScope ME580T-PZ-2L

A trinocular, dual-illumination, polarized-light metallurgical microscope. The -2L designation means it ships with two full objective sets — one for reflected-light metallurgical work (opaque, polished/etched specimens) and one achromatic set shared with transmitted-light use — giving five total magnifications with the standard 10× eyepiece.

Specimen & Microscope Handling

The same setup sequence applies to every module:

  1. Clean the specimen surface with a lint-free lens tissue using a single swipe — never a circular motion.
  2. Handle the specimen by its edges only. Fingerprints etch and will obscure the microstructure.
  3. Place the specimen face-down on the stage. Start at 40× and focus with the coarse knob.
  4. Switch objectives without moving the stage. Use fine focus only above 100×.
  5. Work through magnifications in the order listed for that module, then return the specimen to the labeled tray before leaving class.
Kit rule: return all specimens before leaving class, never clean with abrasives, and notify the instructor immediately if a specimen is damaged. Safety glasses and gloves are required during all lab activities.

Standard copper-alloy etchant. Used on 70/30 brass (Specimen I).
Keller's Reagent Contains Hydrofluoric Acid
HF is dangerously different from the other acids on this page: it can pass through skin with little or no immediate pain, then damage deep tissue and pull calcium out of the bloodstream — a large enough skin contact area can be fatal. Nitrile or neoprene gloves only (not latex), full face shield, and mixing/etching only in a fume hood. Keep calcium gluconate gel at the station and know the emergency procedure before this etchant is opened. All four etchants are corrosive acids — safety glasses, gloves, and adequate ventilation apply across the board, but Keller's reagent specifically needs the HF protocol above.

Etch by swabbing the polished face with a cotton ball soaked in etchant, or brief immersion, per the times noted in the specimen table above. Rinse immediately with ethanol or water and dry with a single swipe of lens tissue.
