Every weekly module in the Semester Metallography Portfolio uses the same shared class microscope and a small set of etchants. This page is a single reference for both: what the EXAMET-5-R-600-HD can actually do, and what each lettered specimen (A–P) needs to be etched with before it goes on the stage.

The Microscope — Unitron EXAMET-5-R-600-HD

A trinocular, reflected-light-only metallurgical microscope. The R in the model name denotes reflected illumination (no transmitted/substage light or polarization); the 600-HD denotes the bundled AU-600-HD digital camera. It ships with a single set of four infinity-corrected objectives, giving four native total magnifications with the standard 10× eyepiece — including 200×, which the previous classroom scope could not produce.

Numerical aperture (NA) sets resolving power, not the objective's marked magnification — higher NA resolves finer detail. On this scope NA rises with magnification (5× NA 0.15 through 50× NA 0.55), so the 50× objective is both the highest-magnification and highest-resolution option — the correct choice for fine features like precipitates and martensite laths.

Specimen & Microscope Handling

The same setup sequence applies to every module:

  1. Clean the specimen surface with a lint-free lens tissue using a single swipe — never a circular motion.
  2. Handle the specimen by its edges only. Fingerprints etch and will obscure the microstructure.
  3. Place the specimen face-down on the stage. Start at 50× and focus with the coarse knob.
  4. Switch objectives without moving the stage. Use fine focus only above 100×.
  5. Work through magnifications in the order listed for that module, then return the specimen to the labeled tray before leaving class.
Kit rule: return all specimens before leaving class, never clean with abrasives, and notify the instructor immediately if a specimen is damaged. Safety glasses and gloves are required during all lab activities.

Etchant Formulas

Nital 2%
2% HNO₃ in ethanol
General-purpose carbon steel etchant. Used on specimens A, B, C.
Marble's Reagent
10 g CuSO₄ + 50 mL HCl + 50 mL H₂O
Reveals austenite grain structure and twin bands in the austenitic stainless grades. Used on 304 (D) and 316 (E). Not suitable for 17-4PH — austenitic-only reagent.
Kalling's No. 2
5 g CuCl₂ + 100 mL HCl + 100 mL ethanol
Reveals the martensitic matrix and precipitate distribution in precipitation-hardening stainless. Used on 17-4PH (Specimen F) only.
Keller's Reagent
2 mL HF + 3 mL HCl + 5 mL HNO₃ + 190 mL H₂O
Standard aluminum etchant. Used on all four aluminum grades: 6061 (G), 6063 (H), 5052 (I), 2024 (J).
Ferric Chloride
5 g FeCl₃ + 50 mL HCl + 100 mL H₂O
Standard copper-alloy etchant — works across both brass and pure/alloyed copper. Used on all three brasses (K, L, M) and both coppers (N, O).
Kroll's Reagent
2–3 mL HF + 4–6 mL HNO₃ + water to 100 mL
Standard titanium etchant. Used on Specimen P (Ti-6Al-4V). Titanium is prone to smearing/embedding during grinding — budget extra time at final polish.
Keller's and Kroll's Reagents Contain Hydrofluoric Acid
HF is dangerously different from the other acids on this page: it can pass through skin with little or no immediate pain, then damage deep tissue and pull calcium out of the bloodstream — a large enough skin contact area can be fatal. Nitrile or neoprene gloves only (not latex), full face shield, and mixing/etching only in a fume hood. Keep calcium gluconate gel at the station and know the emergency procedure before either etchant is opened. All six etchants are corrosive acids — safety glasses, gloves, and adequate ventilation apply across the board, but Keller's and Kroll's specifically need the HF protocol above.

Etch by swabbing the polished face with a cotton ball soaked in etchant, or brief immersion, per the times noted in the specimen table above. Rinse immediately with ethanol or water and dry with a single swipe of lens tissue.
