Copper, Brass & Bronze Designation System

How the UNS/CDA numbering system identifies coppers, brasses, bronzes, and copper-nickels

Copper and its alloys are identified with the Unified Numbering System (UNS), jointly maintained by the Copper Development Association (CDA) and SAE. Every designation is the letter C followed by five digits (Cxxxxx). Unlike steel or aluminum, the digits don't encode a composition percentage — instead, the numbering is organized into blocks, where a five-digit range identifies an entire alloy family (all the brasses, all the phosphor bronzes, and so on). Reading a copper alloy number is a matter of knowing which block a number falls into, not decoding individual digits.

How to Read a UNS Copper Number

The first digit after the C tells you which broad family you're in. Numbers C10000–C79999 are wrought alloys (sheet, bar, tube, wire). Numbers C80000–C99999 are cast alloys — so the same base composition can carry two different numbers depending on whether it was cast or wrought.

Wrought Alloy Family Ranges (C10000–C79999)

Each block of the UNS range corresponds to a distinct alloy family. Memorize the ranges, not individual numbers.

UNS Range Family Composition Characteristics & Typical Uses
C10000–C19999 Coppers & High-Copper Alloys ≥99.3% Cu (coppers); 96–99.3% Cu with small additions (Ag, Te, Be, Cr) for high-copper alloys Best electrical/thermal conductivity of the group. Electrical wire, bus bar, roofing, high-conductivity springs.
C20000–C29999 Brasses — Copper-Zinc Cu-Zn ("yellow brasses") Good formability and moderate strength; color shifts from red toward yellow as Zn content rises. Cartridge cases, plumbing, decorative hardware.
C30000–C39999 Leaded Brasses Cu-Zn-Pb Lead added for free-machining chip control. Screw-machine parts, valves, fittings.
C40000–C49999 Tin Brasses Cu-Zn-Sn Tin improves corrosion resistance over plain brass. Condenser/heat-exchanger tubing, marine hardware (naval brass, admiralty brass).
C50000–C59999 Phosphor Bronzes — Copper-Tin Cu-Sn-P Phosphorus deoxidizes and adds spring temper. Springs, bellows, diaphragms, wear-resistant bearings.
C60600–C64200 Aluminum Bronzes Cu-Al, often + Fe, Ni High strength, wear and corrosion resistance — no tin at all despite the "bronze" name. Marine hardware, gears, heavy-duty bearings.
C64700–C66100 Silicon Bronzes Cu-Si Good weldability and corrosion resistance, mechanical properties similar to mild steel. Architectural work, marine fasteners, welding filler rod.
C66400–C69800 Miscellaneous Copper-Zinc Alloys Cu-Zn + Mn, Al, Si, etc. Alloys that don't fit cleanly elsewhere in the brass ranges, including high-strength "manganese bronze" (which is actually a brass).
C70000–C79999 Copper-Nickels & Nickel Silvers Cu-Ni (copper-nickels); Cu-Ni-Zn (nickel silvers) Outstanding seawater corrosion resistance (copper-nickels); nickel silvers contain no silver at all — the name refers only to their silvery color. Condenser tubing, marine piping, tableware, musical instruments.

Ranges are approximate at the edges — a handful of alloys are grandfathered into a neighboring block for historical reasons. When in doubt, look the specific UNS number up rather than relying on the range alone.

Cast Alloy Ranges (C80000–C99999)

Cast copper alloys follow the same family logic as wrought, in a separate number block. A few of the most common ranges a technician will see on castings and valve bodies:

UNS RangeFamilyTypical Uses
C80000–C81200Cast CoppersHigh-conductivity castings
C83300–C85800Cast Brasses (Red & Leaded)Valve bodies, plumbing fittings — "red brass" / "ounce metal" (e.g. C83600)
C90200–C94500Cast Tin BronzesBearings, bushings, gears, pump/valve components
C95200–C95900Cast Aluminum BronzesHigh-strength wear parts, marine propellers
C96200–C96900Cast Copper-NickelsMarine valve bodies and pump components

Brass vs. Bronze: What the Trade Names Actually Mean

The UNS ranges above are the reliable way to identify an alloy; the everyday trade names "brass" and "bronze" are not. Historically, brass means copper alloyed with zinc, and bronze means copper alloyed with tin. Modern trade usage has drifted from that: aluminum bronze and silicon bronze contain no tin at all, and "manganese bronze" is actually a high-strength brass (Cu-Zn base). When precision matters — ordering material, reading a spec — go by the UNS number, not the trade name.
