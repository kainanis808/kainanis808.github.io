Nital Etch Contains Methanol, Not Ethanol
The SDS shows Nital's diluent as methanol (86–91%), not the ethanol commonly assumed for a "nital" recipe. Methanol is absorbed through skin, is highly flammable (H225), and its SDS carries H370 — causes damage to organs, specifically the optic nerve, with a risk of blindness on significant exposure. Handle it with the same seriousness as the acids on this page: gloves, eye protection, adequate ventilation, and no open flames nearby.
Keller's and Kroll's Reagents Contain Hydrofluoric Acid
HF is dangerously different from the other acids on this page: it can pass through skin with little or no immediate pain, then damage deep tissue and pull calcium out of the bloodstream — a large enough skin contact area can be fatal. Both SDS documents specify calcium gluconate gel as the skin first-aid response. Nitrile or neoprene gloves only (not latex), full face shield, and mixing/etching only in a fume hood. Keep calcium gluconate gel at the station and know the emergency procedure before either etchant is opened.
