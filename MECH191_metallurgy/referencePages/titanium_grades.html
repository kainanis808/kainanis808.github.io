Titanium Grade Designation System

How ASTM grade numbers map to commercially pure and alloyed titanium

Titanium doesn't use a decodable numbering system the way steel or aluminum do. ASTM grade numbers were assigned roughly in the order each composition was standardized, so the number itself carries no information about composition or properties — Grade 6 and Grade 12 are not "more" of anything than Grade 2. To actually know what you're holding, you either look the grade number up in a table, or — more usefully on the job — sort it into one of four metallurgical families (CP/alpha, near-alpha, alpha-beta, beta), because that family is what actually drives strength, weldability, and heat-treat response.

Common ASTM Grades

UNS numbers (Rxxxxx) run in parallel with ASTM grades and appear on mill certs and specs alongside the grade number.

Grade UNS Family Nominal Composition Typical Use
1R50250 CP Titanium 99.5% Ti — softest, most ductile CP grade Chemical processing, medical devices, deep forming
2R50400 CP Titanium 99.2% Ti — the "workhorse" CP grade Marine engineering, desalination, architecture, exhaust systems
3R50550 CP Titanium 99.1% Ti, higher Fe/O than Grade 2 for added strength Industrial equipment needing more strength than Grade 2
4R50700 CP Titanium 99.0% Ti — highest-strength, highest-interstitial CP grade Airframe skin, surgical hardware
5R56400 Alpha-Beta Ti-6Al-4V — accounts for roughly half of all titanium used Aerospace structure and engine parts, medical implants
6R54520 Near-Alpha Ti-5Al-2.5Sn Aircraft structure requiring good weldability and stable high-temp properties
7R52400 Alpha Ti-0.15Pd — Grade 2 + palladium Chemical processing in reducing/crevice-corrosion environments
9R56320 Alpha-Beta Ti-3Al-2.5V — cold-workable, moderate strength Hydraulic tubing, bicycle frames, aerospace tubing
11R52250 Alpha Ti-0.15Pd — Grade 1 + palladium Chemical processing needing Grade 1 formability plus corrosion resistance
12R53400 Near-Alpha Ti-0.3Mo-0.8Ni Heat exchangers, pressure vessels — CP-like weldability, higher strength and elevated-temp capability
23R56401 Alpha-Beta (ELI) Ti-6Al-4V ELI — Extra Low Interstitial version of Grade 5 Medical implants, cryogenic and fracture-critical aerospace parts

"ELI" grades (e.g. Grade 23) hold oxygen and other interstitial elements to a lower maximum than their standard counterpart — same nominal alloy, but with higher ductility and fracture toughness at a slight cost in strength.

Alpha / Near-Alpha / Alpha-Beta / Beta at a Glance

The family — not the grade number — is what tells you how an alloy behaves.

FamilyCrystal StructureHeat Treatable?Key Traits
CP / Alpha HCP No (strength set by composition/interstitials only) Most weldable and most corrosion-resistant family; low-to-moderate strength; good toughness even at cryogenic temperatures. Grades 1–4, 7, 11, 16, 17.
Near-Alpha Mostly HCP, small BCC fraction Limited Retains alpha's weldability while adding creep strength for elevated-temperature service. Grades 6, 12.
Alpha-Beta Mixed HCP + BCC Yes — solution treat & age The most widely used family; heat treatable to a good balance of strength and ductility. Grades 5, 9, 23.
Beta BCC Yes — ages to very high strength Highest strength potential and good cold formability in the solution-treated condition, but higher density than the other families. Used for high-load aerospace fasteners and landing gear (e.g. Ti-10V-2Fe-3Al).

Grades a Technician Sees Most

Grade 2
The default "commercially pure" titanium — if someone says "CP titanium" with no other context, assume this
Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V)
The default titanium alloy — assume this when someone just says "titanium alloy" or "6-4"
Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI)
Grade 5's medical/aerospace-critical cousin — same alloy, tighter interstitial control
