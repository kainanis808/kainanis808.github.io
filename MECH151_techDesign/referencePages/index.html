MECH151 Unit Reference Pages

Plain-language overviews of each course unit for MECH151: Technical Design – Prototyping — what the unit covers, why it matters, and what you'll produce.

Foundations — Before Unit 1

  • Design Fundamentals Defining a design problem with SMART goals and the difference between requirements, objectives, and constraints; evaluating success against measurable criteria; the cyclic design process and why the first version is never the last; the 80/20 rule for knowing when to stop; design intent — the reasoning a model should capture — and how a design is organized, top-down vs. bottom-up.
  • Geometry Basics The common 2D and 3D shape families you'll sketch and model, the elements of a flat shape (point, edge, vertex, area, perimeter), the elements of a solid (vertex, edge, face, volume, surface area), and how the two vocabularies line up dimension by dimension.

Unit 1 — Weeks 4–8

  • CAD Fundamentals (Basic) Sketching and constraints, the geometry of shapes (2D construction, building 3D solids, and measurable properties like area, mass, and moments of inertia), orthographic projection and multiview theory, descriptive geometry, primary/secondary views, section and auxiliary views, dimensioning standards, the first parametric part model, CAD software configuration, and CAD/CAM data translation.

Unit 2 — Weeks 8–11

  • Design for Assembly (DFA) Thread and fastener representation and callouts, assembly modeling with mates and constraints, building assemblies from part models, fasteners in assemblies, methods of joining (heat-set inserts, snap-fits, compliant mechanisms), using online component repositories (McMaster-Carr and others) for standard parts, and DFA design principles.

Unit 3 — Weeks 12–16

  • Design for Manufacturing (DFM) An introduction to tolerancing and fits, why standards carry design intent, an introduction to geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T), manufacturing-ready files, additive and subtractive processes, an introduction to reading and editing G-code, laser cutting, DFM design considerations, machine safety and operation, and the final prototype project.
