Units and Topics

This is a simple page containing the projected units and topics of MECH 151. The course covers Weeks 4–16 (the instructor's period of coverage) and is organized into four units: CAD Fundamentals, CAD Advanced, Design for Assembly, and Design for Manufacturing. Students complete one project-based portfolio module per instructional week. A short Design Fundamentals foundation precedes Unit 1.

Design Fundamentals (Foundations, before Unit 1)

Goal: Step back from the software to ask what a CAD model is for — so that the sketches, features, and assemblies built in Units 1–4 are deliberate and robust.

  • The design process — requirements, concept, detail design, prototype and test, and iteration
  • Design intent — the reasoning a model should capture, and how relations, dimension choices, feature order, and equations capture it
  • Why design intent matters — editability, communication, and downstream reuse in drawings, assemblies, and manufacturing files
  • Organizing a design — top-down vs. bottom-up structure and when each fits
  • Tools for top-down design — layout sketches, skeleton/master models, and in-context references
  • Practices that keep a design robust — fully defined sketches, stable references, simple features, and short dependency chains

Reference page: Design Fundamentals

Unit 1 — CAD Fundamentals (Basic): Sketching, Views, and the First Parametric Model

Goal: Build the drawing and modeling foundation every later unit depends on — how to lay out a controlled sketch, how to project a part into standard views, and how to turn that sketch into a parametric solid model.

  • Sketching fundamentals — lines, arcs, and profiles, and how to keep a sketch clean and editable
  • Sketch constraints and relations — how to fully define a sketch so it behaves predictably when dimensions change
  • Orthographic projection and multiview theory — how a 3D part becomes front, top, and side views
  • Descriptive geometry basics — true length, true shape, and how points, lines, and planes relate in space
  • Primary and secondary views — how to set up and read views projected from other views
  • Basic dimensioning standards — how to place dimensions clearly and follow accepted drawing conventions
  • Introduction to parametric part modeling — sketch, extrude, revolve, and the feature tree
  • Parametric modeling practice — editing features and dimensions and watching the model update

Portfolio focus: Controlled sketches, a multiview drawing, and a first fully parametric part model.
Assessment: Unit 1 Quiz (Week 7).
Reference page: Unit 1 — CAD Fundamentals

Unit 2 — CAD Advanced: Detailed Views, Fasteners, and Production Drawings

Goal: Move from a basic model to a documentation-ready one — the specialized views, thread and fastener callouts, tolerancing, and title-block practices that make a drawing something a shop can build from.

  • Section views — full, half, and offset sections, and when each is the right choice
  • Auxiliary views — projecting a true-shape view of an inclined surface
  • Threads and fasteners — how threads are represented on a drawing and how to write thread and fastener callouts
  • Production and detail drawings — title blocks, revision blocks, and bill-of-materials basics
  • Introduction to tolerancing — limit dimensions, fits, and why tolerance choices matter
  • CAD software configuration — templates, document settings, and drawing standards
  • CAD/CAM data translation — exchanging models between systems with formats such as STEP, IGES, and STL

Portfolio focus: A fully detailed production drawing with section and auxiliary views, fastener callouts, and tolerances.
Assessment: Unit 2 Quiz (Week 10).
Reference page: Unit 2 — CAD Advanced

Unit 3 — Design for Assembly (DFA): Building and Documenting Assemblies

Goal: Combine part models into working assemblies, document them the way a manufacturer expects, and apply the design principles that make an assembly easier and cheaper to put together.

  • Assembly modeling — mates and constraints, and how they position and align components
  • Building assemblies from part models — bringing in components and managing the assembly tree
  • Applying fasteners and threads within assemblies — hardware, hole alignment, and reusable components
  • Assembly drawings — exploded views, balloons, and bills of materials
  • DFA principles — reducing part count, designing for ease of assembly, and accounting for tolerance stack-up
  • Design review and iteration — evaluating an assembly and revising it based on feedback

Portfolio focus: A multi-part assembly model with an exploded assembly drawing and BOM, plus a documented design revision.
Assessment: Unit 3 Quiz (Week 13).
Reference page: Unit 3 — Design for Assembly

Unit 4 — Design for Manufacturing (DFM): From Model to Prototype

Goal: Close the loop from CAD model to physical part — preparing manufacturing-ready files, understanding what each process can and cannot make, and operating shop equipment safely to produce a prototype.

  • Translating parametric models to manufacturing-ready files — toolpaths, export formats, and file checks
  • Additive manufacturing — how 3D printing builds a part and what it does well
  • Subtractive manufacturing — how CNC machining removes material to make a part
  • Laser cutting — sheet-based fabrication and its design constraints
  • DFM considerations — designing parts to suit the chosen process, materials, and cost
  • Machine safety, PPE, and operation — safe setup and use of shop equipment
  • Final prototype project — producing a working prototype from one or more substrates

Portfolio focus: Manufacturing-ready files and a finished prototype built with at least one shop process.
Assessment: Unit 4 Quiz (Week 16); Final Exam (December 14, 2026); Prototype Project and Design Portfolio due at the final exam.
Reference page: Unit 4 — Design for Manufacturing
