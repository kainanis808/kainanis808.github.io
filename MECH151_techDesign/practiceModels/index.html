SolidWorks Practice Problems

Eight levels of official SOLIDWORKS practice models, arranged roughly from easiest (Level 1) to most advanced (Level 8), plus a Level 9 capstone project. Use these to build modeling speed and confidence outside of class.

Attendance Credit

These practice problems are designed to encourage further hands-on practice and build your competency in whichever CAD program you've chosen for this course — not just to satisfy an attendance requirement.

Attendance credit (12 total, 10% of your course grade) is earned weekly by completing and submitting SolidWorks practice-problem work. Work through all 9 levels: complete 3 problems of your choice from each of Levels 1–8 (24 problems), plus the Level 9 final project — a single actuating vice assembly.

  • Within Levels 1–8, you choose which 3 of that level's problems to complete.
  • Each problem PDF gives the target dimensions, suggested tools, and (for higher levels) a design hint.
  • Model the part in whatever CAD program you're using for the course and check it against the given dimensions/volume or center of mass.
  • Credit is contingent on accuracy: your model must actually capture the part's form — its features, proportions, and geometry as shown in the problem — not a placeholder or primitive shape (e.g. a plain block or cylinder) standing in for it. Submissions that don't reflect the modeled part will not receive credit.
  • Email your completed models to kainanis@hawaii.edu to receive credit — either as a direct attachment, or through the UH FileDrop service (recommended for larger CAD files) addressed to the same email — by the end of the semester (December 18, 2026). The weekly deadlines below are recommended pacing.
  • Print or fill out the attendance tracking sheet (DOCX) and include it with your email to record which 3 problems you completed from each of Levels 1–8, plus your Level 9 actuating vice submission.

Level 9 Final Project — Actuating Vice 1 problem · Design + build a working actuating vice assembly

Level 9 is a single capstone project instead of a bank of practice problems: design and model a fully-mated actuating vice assembly — a vice whose jaw opens and closes through an actuating mechanism (e.g. a screw, cam, or lever) rather than being tightened by hand alone.

Model every part of the assembly, mate it so the actuating mechanism moves correctly, and verify the jaws open/close through their full range of motion before submitting.
